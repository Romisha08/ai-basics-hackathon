✳ Project scope · proof of concept

Decode the Vibe

A friendly, responsive website that explains slang, internet expressions, abbreviations, and jargon—then adapts the wording to a chosen audience.

✦ Warm, playful, audience-aware
The unique kernel

Say it differently

Rewrite the same phrase for the selected audience. Compare what “no cap, that was fire” sounds like for a grandparent versus a coworker.

Who it helps

Make the confusing clear

For someone who encounters unfamiliar slang or workplace terms and wants to understand the meaning, tone, and a fitting response.

The inspiration

“What does buddy mean here?”

A friend used “buddy” for a workplace role; the meaning had to be looked up after the conversation.

The feel

Warm and friendly

Clean, playful, and responsive on phones and desktop, with an off-white base and soft lavender and coral accents.

The core loop

Decode the same phrase for another audience to see how the wording changes.

Why this matters

Learn the whole path

Practice planning a simple project and carrying it through to a working app or website—keep it basic, but make it feel original.

The demo moment

One phrase, two vibes

Decode “it’s giving main character energy” for a grandparent, then for a coworker. Show the meaning, likely tone, appropriate use, natural rewrite, and copyable reply. Empty input and decode failure get helpful handling.

NOW What the proof of concept includes
  • Phrase input and audience choices: grandparent, parent, student, coworker.
  • Working AI decode for meaning, likely tone, appropriate use, audience-specific rewrite, and suggested reply.
  • A button to copy the suggested reply.
  • Responsive warm interface, empty-input handling, and a helpful error if decoding fails.
  • Keep AI keys secure. Choose the simplest suitable technology and integration in the technical plan; explain setup and cost before using a paid service.
LATER Ideas for another version

A larger phrase library or additional features can be considered after the basic working experience is complete.

CUT Outside this first version
  • Login, user profiles, or saved history: not needed for a single-session task.
  • A database: no persistent user data is needed.
  • Voice features or screenshot recognition: typing or pasting demonstrates the core idea.
  • Social networking: sharing between users is outside the core experience.
Built around one small experiment ✳ Decode a phrase. Change the audience. Feel the difference.
